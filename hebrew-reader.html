YouWho the app thinks is speaking.

I am

Sets whether the app expects your Hebrew to be masculine or feminine.

Why this matters

Hebrew verbs and adjectives agree with whoever is speaking, so "I want" is ani rotze from a man and ani rotza from a woman. The Sentence pad checks your writing against this. The lens above the Library is the other half: who you're talking to.

Your dataThe only copy of your progress is in this browser.

Export everything

Saves one file holding everything: your library, every word's schedule and strength, your streak and history, banked sentences, path progress, conversations and settings.

What's in it, and what isn't

This is the only real backup — the .xlsx export is words alone. Cached audio and kept voice notes aren't included; they live outside the main save and are regenerated or re-added. The file contains your API keys, so keep it somewhere private.

Import a backup

Replaces everything currently in this browser with a backup file. It doesn't merge.

What happens when you press it

You'll see what's in the file and what it's about to replace, and have to confirm, before anything is written. Merging two drill histories has no correct answer — which due date would win? — so replacing is the honest behaviour. This is also how you move your progress to another browser, another computer, or a phone.

Export a spreadsheet

Your words as Hebrew Vocabulary.xlsx — one sheet laid out like your original table, one row-per-word sheet for sorting and filtering.

Not a backup

This is for reading and printing, not for moving your progress. It holds the words alone — no schedules, streak, banked sentences or path progress. Export everything above is the one that can restore you.

How much room you're using

Counting…

What happens if this storage space fills up

A browser gives each site a fixed storage allowance — around 5MB here — and when it runs out it doesn't warn you: the next save simply fails. Form banks (every conjugation of every verb) are the one thing in the app that grows without limit as you add words, so this is the number to watch. If it ever reaches the ceiling the fix is to move the banks into the browser's larger store, IndexedDB, which is a code change rather than something you can do from this page. Export everything above is the protection in the meantime.

SyncKeep your progress the same on every device.

Sync across devices

Off. Your progress lives only in this browser.

How it works, and what travels

Your progress is kept in a private GitHub repository that only you can read, and merged rather than overwritten — practise on the phone and the laptop and you keep both. Every sync is a commit, so you also get a versioned history you can go back through. Your API keys never leave the device, and neither do your layout, zoom or an in-progress session. Kept voice notes stay on the device that received them: the recordings are too large to travel this way.

Make an empty private repo, then a fine-grained token at github.com/settings/personal-access-tokens — give it access to that one repository only, with Contents: Read and write. Nothing else.

Sync now

Normally it syncs on its own when you open the app and when you finish a session. This forces it — useful if you pick up the phone straight after the laptop.

Turn sync off

Forgets the token on this device. Nothing is deleted — your progress stays here and stays in the repository.

Words and their formsDictionary forms, conjugations, the lens.

Re-file under dictionary forms

Finds words filed under a form rather than the word itself, and moves each to its dictionary form.

What gets moved, and what carries over

"siyamti" instead of "lesayem", or "ha-izdamnut" with the ה still attached. Drill history, marks, pairs and cached audio all move with the word. Only words whose forms have been generated are offered — the model is what tells an attached ה from a word that simply starts with one.

Undo re-filing

Puts the library back exactly as it was before the last re-filing.

What comes back

Drill history, marks and pairs were only repointed, never deleted, so this restores those too. Available until you re-file again.

Fill in forms automatically

When on, the app generates forms for new words on its own once about ten have built up.

What it costs

Roughly two API calls per ten words, from the 500-a-day pool the transcription quota doesn't touch. Off by default so the first run on a fresh library is one you start and watch.

Clear form queue

Unparks words whose forms failed to generate, and resets today's call count.

Why words get parked

A word whose forms fail three times gets parked so the queue stops retrying it forever. Generated forms are kept.

Practice and schedulingWhat you get asked, and when it comes back.

Correct inflated review dates

Pulls any review interval longer than two months back to two months, measured from when you last actually answered that word.

Why this exists

Until 20 Aug 2026 the scheduler counted a word twice when a session showed it twice — as a card and again inside a sentence — so some words were pushed months or years out on a single evening's practice. Your library, strengths, marks and sentences are untouched, and anything already inside two months is left exactly as it is.

Clean up nonsense sentences

Re-checks every banked sentence and removes only the ones that don't actually make sense.

How it decides

The same reviewer that vets new sentences, run retroactively over what's already stored. Everything that passes keeps its history. Different from "Bin the practice sentences", which throws everything away unread.

Bin the practice sentences clears

Deletes every banked practice sentence outright, unread.

What survives

Your library, word strengths and progress all stay exactly as they are — new sentences get written next session.

AudioHebrew voices, and what this key can reach.

Test the microphone

Listens for five seconds and reports exactly what happened — whether the browser can listen at all, whether it heard you, and what it thought you said.

What it checks

Speech recognition is the one part of the app that behaves differently on a phone and a laptop, and when it fails it usually fails silently. This says which of the steps went wrong rather than leaving you guessing: whether the browser supports listening, whether the microphone is held by something else, whether Hebrew recognition is available on this device, and what came back.

Check which Hebrew voices work

Asks Google which speech models your key can actually reach. Uses one request and generates nothing.

Why you'd check

Real Hebrew audio has been failing since 7 Aug. This says whether the app is asking for a model that no longer exists, or the account itself is blocked — rather than guessing.

Generate a test clip

Asks Google to actually speak one short test sentence and plays it back. This is the real thing — unlike Check above, it spends real TTS quota.

Why this and not "Check" above

Checking which models a key can reach only proves the model NAME is valid — it says nothing about whether generation itself succeeds. This is the one test that answers that: if a real voice plays back, real Hebrew audio is working on this key right now, and every Listen / Say-it-back card in Learn will start recording real audio for the words you meet from here on. If it fails, the message is Google's own error text, not a guess.

What it costs

One request against the confirmed real limit — about 3 a minute, 10 a day, per key. It doesn't touch your library or bank; the clip plays once and isn't kept.

AI requestsWhat Google's free tier has left today.

Today's quota

Nothing asked yet today.

Which features spend which pool

Google gives two free models with separate daily allowances: Flash is the stronger one at only 20 requests a day, and Flash-Lite is nearly as good for short answers at 500. Transcribing a voice note, building a conversation, generating a path section and topping up a session use Flash, because those are the jobs where the stronger model earns its cost. Explaining a word, "why is it phrased like that?", the Sentence pad and filling in verb forms all use Flash-Lite. When Flash runs out the app falls back to Flash-Lite by itself rather than failing.

Why this can undercount

This counts requests made in this browser. Google counts them per key, so requests from your phone come out of the same allowance and aren't included here unless the two have synced. It can therefore say you have more left than you do — but never less. The moment Google actually refuses, this says so, because that answer comes from Google rather than from counting.
