## Verdict

This is a better-reasoned codebase than most production software. The comments explain *why*
rather than what, failure modes are anticipated, and several hard calls — separating stability
from difficulty, gating sentences on drilled words, refusing to read Hebrew through an English
voice — are calls most people get wrong.

The criticism below is not "this is amateur." It is that the app has outgrown two founding
assumptions.

**Assumption 1: a word gets reviewed at most once a day.** Nothing enforces it and the session
builder actively violates it. Intervals inflate, so the app reports words as strong on evidence
it collected twice in one sitting. That is `F1`/`F2` and it is the most important thing here.

**Assumption 2: this is a laptop app you double-click.** Everything follows — no sync, no phone,
no backup, and a hard dependency on a Hebrew voice Windows does not ship. Speaking practice that
only happens at a desk is speaking practice that mostly does not happen.

**Performance is not a problem.** At 300 words `renderLibrary()` takes 24ms and `libAll()` takes
0.23ms. Do not spend effort there.

## What is good, and must survive any refactor

- **The Gemini call layer.** Keys-outer / models-inner, 5xx retry, per-minute 429 distinguished
  from daily quota, config-rejection fallback. Textbook, and hard-won.
- **Stability separated from difficulty** with mean reversion, so a bad day cannot destroy weeks.
  The FSRS insight, correctly applied.
- **Hear and Say scored independently.** Most apps conflate these. This one does not.
- **Hints cap the card at "Nearly", and say so up front.** Honest scoring, never a quiet
  downgrade after the fact.
- **The sentence gate.** Refusing to build sentences from undrilled words is right.
- **Every AI feature degrades to copy-paste** rather than blocking. Audio is the sole exception
  and is flagged as such in the UI.
- **Reduced-motion respected in eight places.** Genuinely rare.
- **Storage reads never throw.** `lsGet` resolving rather than rejecting is why a restricted
  browser costs preferences instead of the whole app.

# Group A — Correctness: the scheduler is inflating your intervals

These four compound. Together they mean the strength dot — the number the Learn page, the session
builder, the sentence gate and Conversation mode all read — is measurably more optimistic than
actual recall.

## F1 · CRITICAL · ✅ FIXED (df6f4fb) · A word can be reviewed five times in one evening and the schedule believes all five

`srsApply()` never checks whether the review was actually *due*. It writes `r.last = today` at
line 9239 and reads it nowhere. Every correct answer multiplies stability by
`2.6 - (diff-1) * 0.12` unconditionally.

Eight consecutive correct grades against one fresh word, all in the same second:

```
correct #4  →  stab  18.1   due 2026-09-07
correct #5  →  stab  40.7   due 2026-09-30
correct #6  →  stab  94.0   due 2026-11-22
correct #7  →  stab 223.3   due 2027-03-31
correct #8  →  stab 365.0   due 2027-08-20  (cap)
```

The README says the sequence runs "1 → 2 → 5 → 12 → 28 days", which is true *per review* — but
nothing makes a review take a day.

**Fix.** In `srsApply`, if `r.last === today`, record the answer (bump `n`, `miss`, adjust `diff`)
but leave `stab` and `due` alone. A miss on a repeat should still shrink stability — failing twice
in one session is real evidence. Succeeding twice is not.

**Done in `df6f4fb`, with two deliberate departures from the above:**

1. **The migration is a Settings button, not an on-open migration.** Every other row in Settings
   states its effect before you press it. A scheduler rewrite that ran silently the first time you
   opened a new build would be the one action on irreplaceable data you never got to refuse. It
   lives under Resets as *"Correct inflated review dates"* and names the count before running.
2. **The "honest ceiling" criterion was dropped.** The proposed test — an interval beyond
   `2.6^(n-1)` is unearnable — reaches 803 days by the eighth answer, so it caught nothing at all.
   Deriving a real ceiling needs calendar time, and a schedule record doesn't carry the date the
   word was first practised. Replaced with a flat 60-day cap: longer than the app has existed, so
   nothing under it can be pathological, and the deliberate 30-day band-setting from *Mark Duolingo
   batch as known* is left alone — undoing a user's own declaration of what they know would be a
   worse bug than the one being fixed.

**Measured on the real 2026-08-20 backup:** 17 records corrected, 0 left inflated, due-today count
unchanged at 75, idempotent on a second run. One regression was caught only because it was run
against real data: `toda` carried stability 110 *and* a due date four days in the past, and
recomputing from `last` alone would have delayed an overdue word by two months. `srsDeflate` now
never moves a due date later than it already was.

## F2 · CRITICAL · ✅ FIXED (df6f4fb) · The session builder puts the same word in twice on purpose

F1 would be theoretical if sessions never repeated a word. They do. The tail of `learnBuild()`
(line ~10553) tops up short sessions and de-duplicates only against other `word` cards — not
against `covered`, the map of words already appearing inside sentence and shadow cards.

Built against an 8-word library, the early-game case this fallback exists for:

```
13 cards from 8 words
repeated within one session:  בית · גדול · מים · רוצה
that is half the library, graded twice each
```

Add `learnCommit()`, which calls `srsApply` for *every* content token in a sentence, and a word
sitting in two banked sentences gets two more grades. With F1 active, a word at a 5-day interval
answered correctly twice in one sitting lands at ~25 days.

**Fix.** Fixing F1 defuses this on its own. Still change the tail loop to check
`covered[targets[i]]` as well — a word met inside a sentence and again as a bare card in the same
15 cards is thin practice regardless of scoring.

## F3 · SERIOUS · ✅ FIXED (df6f4fb) · A word's first card is an impossible test, scored as a lapse

There is no "meet the word" step outside the Path. On a fresh library of 178 words, card 1 of
session 1 was *"Say this word in Hebrew: hot"* — for a word never shown, defined or spoken. You
press Missed, and the scheduler treats it as a lapse rather than an introduction.

```
1st miss  →  diff 6.100   n 1  miss 1   "progressing"
2nd miss  →  diff 7.145   n 2  miss 2   "weak"        ← locked out
+correct  →  diff 6.438   n 3  miss 2   "weak"
+correct  →  diff 5.766   n 4  miss 2   "weak"
+correct  →  diff 5.128   n 5  miss 2   "weak"
+correct  →  diff 4.521   n 6  miss 2   "progressing" ← 4 reps to recover
```

Because `wordReady()` requires `progressing` or better, a word you simply had not been taught yet
is barred from sentence generation and Conversation mode for four more correct answers. This is
the real mechanism behind "expect no sentences for the first few sessions" — not only foundation
building, but a self-inflicted penalty.

**Fix.** When `r.n === 0`, treat the card as an introduction: show the answer, do not grade it,
set `stab = 1`, `due = tomorrow`, leave `diff` at baseline. Ideally pair with a real card kind —
Hebrew, pronunciation and English shown together, spoken once, no grade buttons.

## F4 · SERIOUS · ✅ FIXED (df6f4fb) · `miss` is a lifetime counter that never decays

`srsStrength` calls a word weak when `miss / n >= 0.4`. But `miss` only ever increases; the ratio
falls solely by growing `n`. Two fumbles in week one still weigh on that word in November, and a
word now known cold reads as weak until it has ~5x as many correct answers as it ever had misses.

**Fix.** Decay it — `r.miss *= 0.9` on each correct answer — or score the last 10 outcomes from a
small rolling array instead of lifetime totals. The second is more faithful and costs ~10 bytes
per word.

## F5 · WORTH DOING · ✅ FIXED (df6f4fb) · No interval fuzz, so each session's words come due together forever

`addDays` is exact. Fifteen words drilled tonight all return in exactly 2 days, then 5, then 12 —
as a clump, permanently. Over months this builds review days three times heavier than their
neighbours, and the 50% overdue cap then defers the overflow into a growing backlog.

**Fix.** One line: scatter the due date by ±15% of the interval —
`Math.round(stab * (0.85 + Math.random() * 0.3))`. Every serious SRS does this.

# Group B — Fragility and waste

## F6 · CRITICAL · ✅ FIXED (96206c7) · Nothing is backed up, and writes fail in silence

`lsSet` swallows every exception, including `QuotaExceededError`. When storage fills, the app
carries on looking perfectly healthy while nothing is saved. The comment above it says a
restricted environment "costs you your saved preferences and nothing else" — true when that
function guarded preferences. It now guards the entire drill history.

There is no export or import of state anywhere. The only export is `Hebrew Vocabulary.xlsx` —
words only. Not covered: SRS history, streak, stats, banked sentences, path progress,
conversations, focus marks, aliases, form banks. Sixty independent keys, no schema version, no
atomic write.

One "clear browsing data", one profile reset, one new laptop, and months of it are gone with no
warning and no recovery.

**Fix.** Make `lsSet` return a boolean and surface a real banner on failure. Add
**Export everything** / **Import** — every `hvr_*` key into one versioned JSON file. That export
is also the migration path needed for the phone (F15), so it pays for itself twice.

## F7 · SERIOUS · The storage ceiling was found and answered with a console warning

`formsSizeGuard()` at line 3079 warns when `hvr_library` passes 1MB, with an honest comment:
*"Form banks are the first thing in this app that grows without bound."* It fires `console.warn`,
from exactly one call site, in an app opened by double-click where DevTools is never open.

Twenty-one forms per verb plus `formsMeta` state per tag, all inside the same key as the library.
At 300 words with banks, expect 300–500KB in that one key alone, before the 400-sentence bank and
cached path content.

**Fix.** Move `forms` out of `hvr_library` into IndexedDB — the store already exists for audio and
`audioPut`/`audioGet` are the pattern to copy. Until then, at minimum make that warning visible in
Settings next to the existing audio count.

## F8 · SERIOUS · Six features fight over 20 requests a day while a 500-a-day model idles

The split is recorded in the file's own comments: `gemini-flash-latest` at 20/day,
`gemini-flash-lite-latest` at 500/day. Only three call sites use the fast pool.

| Call site | Pool | Needs the strong model? |
|---|---|---|
| `transcribeAudio` | 20/day | Yes — audio is genuinely hard |
| `learnTopUp` | 20/day | Arguable — a review pass already follows |
| `learnMakeConversation` | 20/day | Arguable |
| `pathGenerateContent` | 20/day | Arguable |
| `learnExplainWord` | 20/day | No — short explanation |
| `learnWhy` | 20/day | No — short explanation |
| `padAskExplain` | 500/day | Already correct |
| `learnReviewItems` | 500/day | Already correct |
| `formsAsk` | 500/day | Already correct |

Three voice notes, a conversation, a path section and two "why is it phrased like that?" taps and
the day is gone. And the strong pool dying just falls through to lite anyway — so lite is *already*
deemed good enough for all of these. It is used as an emergency instead of as policy.

**Fix.** Route by task, not by panic. Move `learnExplainWord` and `learnWhy` to
`GEMINI_MODELS_FAST` — they are short teaching notes, exactly what `padAskExplain` already sends
there. Leave generation on the strong model but let it fall back after one attempt. That alone
roughly doubles what a day of use supports.

## F9 · SERIOUS · Real Hebrew audio may not be broken — it may be pinned to a retired model

`GEMINI_TTS_MODELS = ["gemini-2.5-flash-preview-tts"]`. A single, dated, *preview* model name,
with no fallback list.

Fifteen hundred lines earlier the same file states the rule: *"Always use -latest aliases; dated
names get retired underneath you"* — a lesson learned when exactly that happened to a text model.
The TTS path never got that policy. The README concluded on 2026-08-07 that Google's TTS was
broken for the account; as far as this review can tell, no other model name was tried before
reaching that conclusion.

**Fix.** Before rebuilding anything: make `GEMINI_TTS_MODELS` a list, add current TTS model names,
and hit `ListModels` with the key to see what actually exists. A 20-minute test that could restore
an entire feature — the audio caching, background generation and IndexedDB store behind it are all
still present and working.

## F10 · SERIOUS · No Hebrew voice means two of six drills silently cease to exist

`learnBuild` sets `wantListen = voice && shape !== "speaking"`, and shadow cards need a voice too.
On a stock Windows machine — which ships no Hebrew voice — **Hear and answer** and **Say it back**
are dropped from every session. The banner explains the install, but the start screen does not say
which drills are currently missing.

The reasoning for withholding them is right (a listening drill without audio is a reading drill in
a coat). The problem is that this is the app's biggest silent degradation and it is reported as a
footnote.

**Fix.** Two lines on the start screen: "2 of 6 drill types are off — no Hebrew voice." And solve
it properly via F9 and F16.

## F11 · WORTH DOING · Three prefix tables that have drifted apart

The same linguistic fact — Hebrew clitic prefixes — is encoded four times in three incompatible
shapes:

```
PREFIX1      (3702)  7 letters, reading direction
PREFIX2      (3701)  כש only — reading direction
AI_PREFIXES  (3373) 14 pairs, requires a hyphen in the translit
PAD_PREFIXES (7056) 15 pairs, writing direction
```

They disagree in ways with consequences. `PAD_PREFIXES` maps `"ma" → מ`, which the others do not,
so an unknown word starting "ma" can be mis-split. `PREFIX2` knows `כש` (*kshe-*) but the pad does
not, so the app can read "when I…" and cannot let you write it.

**Fix.** One table: Hebrew letter → array of accepted spellings, with a flag for hyphen-required.
Derive all four current uses from it. A contained refactor with obvious tests, and the clearest
example of the "built bit by bit" seam.

## F12 · WORTH DOING · The tests cover the newest feature and nothing else

139 tests, all passing. Every one covers word forms, the lens, agreement, clitics or rebasing —
the work from the last two days. Nothing tests `srsApply`, `learnBuild`, `bankServable`,
`wordFamiliarity`, the pad's skeleton matching, or conversation grading.

That is why F1 and F3 survived. They live in pure, easily-tested functions — `srsApply` takes a
plain object and returns one — and the harness to test them already exists and is well built.

**Fix.** Write the scheduler tests *before* the Group A fixes, so each fix is demonstrated rather
than assumed. Roughly 15 tests covers all of F1–F5.

## F13 · WORTH DOING · Housekeeping that is starting to cost

- **24 backup copies, ~15MB, in a synced Drive folder, and no git repo.** Version control is being
  hand-rolled by filename and Drive is paying to sync every copy. `git init` replaces all of it and
  gives real diffs.
- **A stale comment at line 8589** states microphone input is impossible here — "Chrome refuses
  microphone access to `file://` origins, so there is no recording or recognition to be had." The
  mic feature was built anyway and works. Anyone reading that block will believe the app cannot do
  something it does.
- **`speakerGet()` reads a key nothing ever writes.** Speaker gender is permanently `"m"` with no
  UI — fine in practice, but it is presented in the comments as a setting and is not one.
- **Six unused functions:** `audioClearAll`, `convoAddScene`, `focusStar`, `formsAutoOn`,
  `openLibEditor`, `pendingCount`.
- **No `window.onerror`.** A throw mid-render leaves a half-drawn screen and no message. In a
  single-file app with no console open, that reads as "the app is broken" with zero diagnostic.

## F14 · WORTH DOING · Two different app states depending on how it is opened

Opened by double-click, the origin is `file://`. Opened through the dev server in
`.claude/launch.json`, the origin is `localhost:8777` — a different origin, a different
localStorage, an empty library. It looks like the app has wiped itself.

(This is also why the testing behind this review never touched the real data.)

**Fix.** Detect an empty library on a non-`file:` origin and say so plainly, pointing at Import
(F6).

# Group C — What is missing

## F15 · CRITICAL · It is not on the phone, and speaking practice lives on phones

No manifest, no service worker, no sync. The app is a file on one laptop. Every design note about
phone layouts describes a screen the app cannot currently reach.

This is the biggest gap in the product and it is not close. A five-minute speaking drill competes
for the ten minutes on a bus, in a queue, waiting for a kettle — not for a deliberate sit-down at
a desk. Everything else in this document is worth less than being able to open this on a phone.

It also unlocks the microphone properly: `getUserMedia` is restricted on `file://` and fully
supported on HTTPS, so speech grading would stop being fragile and stop re-prompting.

**Fix.** Publish the single file to any static host (GitHub Pages is free and fits an app that is
literally one file), add a web app manifest and a cache-first service worker, and Add to Home
Screen. It stays one file, still offline, still no backend. The one hard part is moving existing
progress across — which the export/import from F6 solves.

## F16 · CRITICAL · The app throws away grandad's voice, then tries to synthesise a replacement

The finding to act on first after the scheduler.

`transcribeAudio()` reads the voice note to base64, sends it to Google, takes the transcript, and
lets the audio fall out of scope. It is never stored.

Meanwhile the app spends real effort trying to produce Hebrew audio: a TTS integration that is
currently broken (F9), an IndexedDB cache built for it, a background generation queue, a
rate-limit pacer, and a fallback to a flat Windows voice the README itself calls uncomfortable to
practise against.

Authentic native-speaker Hebrew — correct prosody, real conversational speed, the actual accent
that needs to be understood, saying things that actually matter — is being discarded, and quota is
then spent approximating it badly.

**Fix.** Keep the blob. `audioPut()` already exists. Store each voice note under its message id,
and store per-sentence offsets if transcription returns them. Then **Hear and answer** and **Say
it back** run on real audio instead of a synthetic voice; F10 stops mattering because listening
drills no longer need an installed voice; and the Library's 🔊 can prefer a real clip of that word
in context over any TTS. Cost: one `audioPut` call and some IndexedDB. Payoff: the listening half
of the app becomes better than anything purchasable.

## F17 · SERIOUS · No mistake review — the session ends and the misses evaporate

Grades go into the scheduler and nowhere else. There is no "here are the six you missed tonight"
screen, no way to look back at yesterday's, no list of the words that keep costing you. The Learn
page shows aggregate bands over a fortnight; it cannot answer "what did I get wrong just now."

For a speaking app this is the highest-value cheap feature after the scheduler fixes: the moment
right after a miss is when the correction sticks.

**Fix.** The end-of-session screen already exists. Add the missed cards to it — Hebrew,
pronunciation, English, a replay button, and a "drill these now" button that builds a session from
exactly those. No storage schema change needed; the session object is still in memory at that
point.

## F18 · SERIOUS · No dark mode, in an app for evening practice

Zero `prefers-color-scheme` rules. The palette is a fixed warm paper `#f5f0e7`. Voice notes from
family arrive in the evening and that is when they get opened; a full-brightness cream screen is
the wrong surface for it, and worse on a phone once F15 lands.

**Fix.** The CSS is already fully tokenised at `:root` — genuinely a single `@media` block
redefining ~20 custom properties, plus care that gold still reads as achievement on a dark ground.

## F19 · WORTH DOING · A reload mid-session loses the session

`learnSession` is an in-memory variable. It survives switching tabs within the app (`learnEnter`
resumes rather than restarts — good) but not a refresh, a crash, or closing the tab. Per-card
stats are already written as you go, so nothing is mis-counted; the session just cannot be picked
back up.

**Fix.** Persist `{cards, i, marks}` to one key on each advance and offer "resume" on the start
screen. Matters much more once this is on a phone, where the OS discards backgrounded tabs freely.

## F20 · WORTH DOING · Three content systems that do not know about each other

Free-form sessions, Conversations and the Path each generate their own material, with their own
prompts, their own caches (`hvr_bank`, `hvr_convo`, `hvr_pathcontent`) and their own quality rules.
Only the free-form bank gets the second-opinion review pass. Only the Path can raise the grammar
ceiling above present tense.

The consequence: sentences met on the Path never reach the review pass, and conversations cannot
use the tense the Path has just taught.

**Fix.** Not urgent, and not to be restructured now. When generation is next touched, make grammar
level a parameter of one shared generator rather than a property of which system called it.

# The plan

Ordered so each phase makes the next one safe. Phase 0 exists so a mistake in Phase 1 cannot cost
anything, and its export is also what makes Phase 3 possible.

## Phase 0 — Stop being able to lose it

*Half a day. Covers F6, F13. Nothing else should be touched first.*

1. `git init` in the folder, commit the current file, and delete the 24 `BACKUP-*` copies once the
   first commit exists.
2. Settings → **Export everything**: walk every `hvr_*` key into one JSON blob with a `schema`
   version, download it. Plus **Import**, which validates the version, previews what it will
   replace, and confirms before writing.
3. Make `lsSet` return `false` on failure. Add one global `storageFailed` banner that appears the
   moment any write fails and does not go away.
4. Add `window.onerror` → a visible red bar with the message and a Copy button.

## Phase 1 — Make the scheduler honest

*One day. Covers F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F12. Tests first — these are pure functions and the harness
already exists.*

1. Write ~15 `T(...)` cases against `srsApply` asserting the wanted behaviour: a same-day repeat
   does not grow `stab`; a first exposure is not a lapse; `miss` decays; intervals scatter. Watch
   them fail.
2. **F1:** in `srsApply`, branch on `r.last === today` — update `n`, `miss`, `diff`; leave `stab`
   and `due` untouched on success. A miss still shrinks `stab`.
3. **F3:** branch on `r.n === 0` — no lapse, no difficulty penalty, `stab = 1`, `due = tomorrow`.
4. **F4:** `r.miss *= 0.9` on a correct answer.
5. **F5:** ±15% fuzz on the computed due date.
6. **F2:** the tail loop in `learnBuild` checks `covered`, not just existing word cards.
7. Add a one-time migration that rescales existing `stab` values — anything over ~60 days was
   probably earned under the inflated rule. Halving everything above 60 is crude but closer to true
   than leaving it.

## Phase 2 — Real Hebrew audio, from the person you are learning it for

*One day. Covers F16, F9, F10. The highest-value feature in this document.*

1. In `transcribeAudio`, before the request, `audioPut()` the file under a message id. Store the id
   alongside the transcript in history.
2. Ask the transcription prompt for per-sentence timings. If they come back, slice; if not, the
   whole clip is still far better than nothing.
3. When building `listen` and `shadow` cards, prefer a real clip. Drop the `hasHeVoice()` gate
   whenever one exists — those drills should run on a stock Windows machine.
4. Separately: make `GEMINI_TTS_MODELS` a list, call `ListModels` with the key, and find out
   whether TTS is actually dead or just pointed at a retired preview name.
5. Say on the start screen which drills are active and which are not, and why.

## Phase 3 — Get it on the phone

*One to two days. Covers F15, F19. Changes how often the app is opened more than any feature.*

1. Push the file to a GitHub Pages repo. One file, one commit, HTTPS for free.
2. Add `manifest.webmanifest` (name, icons, `display: standalone`, the paper background) and a
   cache-first service worker so it works underground.
3. Export from the laptop, import on the phone. That is the migration, and Phase 0 already built
   it.
4. Persist the in-flight session (F19) — mobile browsers discard background tabs.
5. Re-check the mic: `getUserMedia` on HTTPS remembers permission, so the per-session re-prompt
   disappears.

## Phase 4 — Spend the quota where it earns something

*Half a day. Covers F8, F7. Roughly doubles what a day of use supports.*

1. Move `learnExplainWord` and `learnWhy` to `GEMINI_MODELS_FAST`. Two one-word changes.
2. Add a request ledger — the same shape as `formsQAll`, but for every call — and show "4 of your
   20 strong-model requests used today" in Settings. You cannot budget what you cannot see.
3. Reserve a couple of strong-model requests for transcription so a Path section cannot eat the
   budget for a voice note arriving later.
4. Move `forms` out of `hvr_library` into IndexedDB, and surface `formsSizeGuard` in Settings
   instead of the console.

## Phase 5 — The things that make it pleasant

*A day, spread out. Covers F17, F18, F11, F13. Cheap, visible, safe in any order.*

1. **Mistake review** on the end-of-session screen, with a "drill these now" button.
2. **Dark mode** — one media block over the existing tokens.
3. **One prefix table**, with the other three derived from it, and tests.
4. Delete the six dead functions, fix the stale mic comment at line 8589, and either give
   `speakerGet` a setting or hardcode it honestly.

# What to deliberately not do

**Do not split the file.** Single-file, double-click, no build step is a real feature and the
reason this app exists at all. 13,428 lines is large but well-sectioned, and the comments carry the
reasoning. Splitting it buys tidiness and costs the thing that makes it what it is.

**Do not optimise rendering.** Measured because it was asked: nothing is slow. `renderLibrary()` is
24ms at 300 words and `libAll()` is 0.23ms despite 80 call sites. Caching the library in memory
would add an invalidation bug for no user-visible gain.

**Do not add more session shapes or settings.** The file's own note is right — the failure mode of
a configurable drill app is spending the practice window configuring it. Five shapes is already one
more than the four that note defends.

**Do not restructure the three content systems yet (F20).** A genuine design smell, but the Path
has not been walked start to finish once. Use it first; the right shared abstraction will be
obvious afterwards and guessed wrong now.

## Method

Reviewed against `hebrew-reader.html` as of 2026-08-20 12:11. The self-test suite was run in a real
browser (`?selftest=1`): 139 passed, 0 failed. Findings F1, F2 and F3 were reproduced by executing
the app's own functions against synthetic stores. Timings were measured over 10–200 iterations at a
300-word library. All testing ran on a local server origin, so the `file://` library the app
normally uses was never touched.
